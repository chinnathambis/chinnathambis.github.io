01 — The Problem

The product was easy to find. The right variant wasn’t.

Search “original donut shop regular” and the result surfaces one version: the 32-count pack of Regular K-Cup pods. Other sizes and flavours existed, but the only sign of them was a line of plain text: “Options: 4 sizes, 3 flavors.”

Customers who wanted a different size had to land on the wrong one first, then find their way to the right one. The sizes were in the catalogue; Search just didn’t show them.

02 — The Signal

People understood the idea. They just didn’t see it.

Usability research findings. These are research signals, not business metrics.

~50%of participants missed the existing variation message
80%+clicked the intended area across tested concepts
4.58/5highest clarity rating among concepts

The existing message worked when people noticed it, but about half of them didn’t. When the concept was made visible, people understood it and acted on it.

04 — The Decision
The call

Discovery in Search.
Selection in a focused surface.

Search answers

“Are there other variations?”

Bottom sheet answers

“Which variation do you want?”

Search answers one question and the sheet answers the other, so neither has to do both.

05 — The Solution

A signal on the result. A sheet for the choice.

The Search result carries a small, explicit signal that other variations exist. Tapping it opens a focused bottom sheet to choose one, then continue to details or cart without losing your place in Search.

Final design — launched

07 — Validation & Outcome
